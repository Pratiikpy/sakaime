# Sakaime deck: speaker notes

One entry per slide of `sakaime-deck.pdf`, in order. Generated by `build-deck.mjs` from the slide sources; edit the notes in `slides/`, not here.

## 1. Sakaime

Open on the sentence, then the bar. Sakaime checks whether the evidence behind a claim covers the exact use a buyer has in mind. The first use is a hospital team reviewing a medical-AI vendor's claim. Say once, up front, what is real and what is constructed: the regulator, registry and literature records are real and carry retrieval dates; every vendor claim and the hospital requirement are constructed and labelled so on screen; the product on these slides is running in sample mode, which replays recordings made against the real services on 2026-10-27 while the compiler, identity scorer, verifiers and guard run for real.

## 2. A claim can be true and still be wrong for you.

The problem in one line: a claim can be true and still be wrong for you. These are three claims about the same cleared product, EyeArt 2.1.0, taken from the recorded run. Each is constructed by us in the style of a vendor claim. In each, one part of the use does not match what the evidence covers: version 3.0 against a study of 2.1.0; ages 18 to 21 against a regulator summary that analysed ages 22 and older; an emergency department against primary and eye care. A tool that only checks that the evidence exists says yes to all three. The hospital's question is different: does it cover my use. The same product also has a dimension that passes: the date of use sits inside the authorization period.

## 3. A reviewer must decide what a vendor claim covers.

Impact is the criterion worth most, so be exact about the user. The user is a hospital AI-evaluation reviewer: a clinical informatics analyst, procurement analyst or quality lead on an AI intake committee. A vendor packet arrives; the committee needs a defensible, source-linked account of what the evidence does and does not establish before the technical and clinical review. These are the five jobs the product is built around. Be plain about what is not measured: no hospital reviewer or buyer has been interviewed, so demand and willingness to use are not verified, and nothing in this deck is evidence of demand.

## 4. Five systems, five identifiers that rarely cross.

Why this is a Track 1 problem. The evidence for one product lives in five places that keep different identifiers: the regulator keys on a K or De Novo number, the registry on an NCT id, the literature on a PMID or DOI, the vendor on a product name and a version string, the hospital on its own case id. We probed twelve real AI-enabled devices with no keys. Only 3 of 12 had an identifier that crosses between the device record and a study. Only 1 of 12, EyeArt, had a study that states the software version the regulator reviewed. Six of twelve names collide with something unrelated, and eleven of twelve show a version or identity conflict between at least two sources. So linking has to be shown as ranked candidates with reasons, never as fact below an exact identifier. One reader classified these links; no second reader.

## 5. Lookup tools return evidence, not verdicts.

An honest competitor slide. We read eighteen products and frameworks; none of them checks a vendor claim against version, population, setting and use. The two closest are evidence lookup tools: Constat accepts one K or De Novo number and returns extracted fields with quotes, none of its fourteen tools takes claim text; HealthIT.com publishes a ledger with no claim input. Given the same 26 claims, a careful reviewer could re-derive our answer key from Constat's output for 7 of 26 and from HealthIT's for 0 of 26. Neither gives a verdict. The stronger alternative is a generic model given all the records. That arm labels more claims exactly right than we do, and it also labels 20 to 39 percent of unsupported claims as supported. The Gate 0 rule says the gap stands, but that is a weak statement: Sakaime's own exact accuracy does not reach 0.80, the claims and the key were written by one builder, and Constat and HealthIT were not run on the held-out claims.

## 6. Claims in. Labelled, sourced, signed answers out.

What we built, on one page. In: the hospital's intended use and the vendor's claims. Out: for every claim one of five labels, the smallest mismatch, the document that would settle each unknown, and a signed record. In between are five things the product does by itself: discover and onboard sources, join them with one federated query, resolve which records describe the same product, study and version, read the documents with verified quotes, and compile the answer dimension by dimension in deterministic code. A web workstation sits on top with thirteen screens, and the whole run is replayable offline.

## 7. One real claim: question, sources, join.

Now one real claim through every step, so you can see what we do and how. The case is EyeArt 2.1.0, cleared as K200667. Step 1, the question: the hospital's intended use and ten constructed vendor claims; the reviewer asks whether the evidence behind a sensitivity of at least 85 percent applies to primary-care screening of adults. Step 2, discover: the guard first reads the request and lets it proceed; the discoverer finds nine registered source cards and the plan uses six: three regulator records, the registry, the literature and the hospital policy. Step 3, join: one federated statement joins the hospital database, the registry snapshots and the vendor packet files in Zetaris; three statements in this case returned 10, 10 and 3 rows from recorded answers of the real engine. Nothing is joined in application memory.

## 8. Identity, quote, compile.

Step 4, resolve identity. The regulator number K200667 is the anchor. The dossier holds six typed links. Four are CONFIRMED because an exact identifier crosses or a reviewer decided: the same submission in the device database, the study in PubMed, the registry entry NCT03112005, and the paper reporting that registry entry. One registry entry, NCT04302012, is only a CANDIDATE with a score of 4.3 bits, because the version is stated on one side only. The marketed-version link is AMBIGUOUS. A score can never produce CONFIRMED. Step 5, extract: this is a real sentence from the FDA summary on page 7: the pivotal analysis covered persons aged 22 years and older. Every number and quote is anchored to a page, checked to exist in the stored record by code, and separately checked for whether it supports the assertion. Step 6, compile: seven dimensions each come out contained, partial, disjoint or unknown. For the claim about version 3.0, version is disjoint.

## 9. Label, break it on purpose, sign it.

Step 7, label. The compiler's table turns the dimensions into one of five labels. For the version-3.0 claim the answer is not enough evidence, the smallest mismatch is version 3.0 against 2.1.0, and the product writes the request that would settle it: study results for version 3.0, or a written change-control statement. Across the ten vendor claims the recorded run gives one supported, three supported only for a narrower use, one contradicted, two not enough evidence and three that need domain review. Step 8, drill: the study source is switched off through the real code. Every finding that relied on it drops to not enough evidence; the one supported claim stops being supported; nothing becomes supported because something was lost. Step 9, dossier: the whole run is exported as one signed bundle with a verifier that recomputes hashes and reruns the compiler. A signature shows integrity, not truth, and this key is a demo key.

## 10. Nine layers. Code decides; Nemotron proposes.

The architecture is nine layers and one rule. The rule: deterministic code owns calculations, labels and checks; Nemotron proposes, and a model proposal never becomes a fact until a deterministic check or a reviewer accepts it. Layer 0 is the guard, which reads the request and every document-derived instruction. Layer 1 discovers and onboards sources. Layer 2 is Zetaris federation. Layer 3 resolves identity as typed links. Layer 4 extracts with verified quotes; long documents go through the Markovian reader. Layer 5 is evidence memory: the bi-temporal World Model and H-MEM. Layer 6, the applicability compiler, uses no model at all. Layer 7 is the bounded investigation loop with its roles. Layer 8 is the signed dossier and the offline verifier. Sponsor technology sits where it changes outputs: Zetaris at layers 1 and 2, Meterless engines at layers 0, 4 and 5, Nemotron through Nightshift at the roles.

## 11. One language, typed at every boundary.

The stack, read from package.json and the Zetaris gate record. One language end to end: TypeScript on Node, with Zod schemas checked at every boundary, because the Meterless engines and harnesses are TypeScript. SQLite through the built-in node module for local state, Hono for the HTTP API, DuckDB only as an independent test oracle for the Zetaris join. The web app is React with Vite. Verification uses Vitest, fast-check property tests, Playwright and axe. The Zetaris engine is the 2.4.3.1 hackathon build in Docker with Postgres and file silos beside it; Nemotron is reached through Nightshift. Licence is Apache-2.0.

## 12. Discover, work across, reconcile.

Track 1 asks for a clever ability to discover and work across fragmented data sources. The organizers' own good example is an agent that queries three disconnected systems, reconciles conflicting records and produces one sourced answer showing which system each fact came from. Sakaime does each of those three things and shows each on screen. Discover: a source nobody wrote an adapter for goes through eight stages, and an address inside a network or at cloud metadata is refused at stage one. Work across: one federated Zetaris statement joins a hospital database, registry and regulator snapshots and vendor files, and every column is tied to its source. Reconcile: identity is typed links with states, authority depends on the kind of assertion, and every finding names the exact passage, page and retrieval date it rests on.

## 13. It answers no more broadly than the evidence allows.

Innovation, in the judges' words: how original or creative is the approach. Four ideas, none of them a wrapper around a prompt. One: a claim is checked dimension by dimension against what the evidence covers, with three values, so unknown is never read as covered. Two: the answer is the smallest mismatch plus the exact document that would settle it. Three: a counterfactual lab, where changing the version, the ages, the setting or the date recomputes the answer with no model call. Four: change-triggered re-evaluation, where only the findings that depended on a changed fact are marked stale. Five: source onboarding that checks meaning, not only types. The honest comparison is on slide 30: against generic Nemotron arms we are less often exactly right, and we are the only arm family that never labels an unsupported claim as supported.

## 14. New sources are checked for meaning, then registered.

Discovery is the centrepiece of Track 1. A new source goes through eight stages and stops at the first one it fails, with the reason shown: authorization against an allowlist with an SSRF-safe fetch, capability discovery through the Zetaris catalog or the source card, a bounded probe that fingerprints the schema, a mapping proposal from Nemotron, a deterministic semantic check, human review for anything uncertain, registration, and monitoring for drift. We tested it on 102 mutated layouts built from four tables, using a sealed answer file whose hash the runner checks. The first run on the sealed suite mapped 39 of 48 mappable layouts and caught 44 of 44 layouts that must not register, with no false accepts. We then fixed what that run showed, and the second run, on the same cases, is 48 of 48 and 44 of 44; those cases are no longer held out, and the slide says so. A hardcoded loader maps 22 of 48 and catches 12 of 44. Turning the model off or the cross-source joins off each costs measurable outcomes. Not independent: the builder wrote the cases and the labels.

## 15. Link what is exact. Keep the rest uncertain.

Identity is a graph of typed links, not one merged record. A shared submission number establishes that two records describe one authorization and nothing more. Each link has a state: confirmed only by an exact identifier or a reviewer, candidate with a score, ambiguous, or rejected. The picture is a real ambiguous link from the Transpara case: a study that reports software version 1.7.0 against the cleared 1.7.2. The waterfall shows how the evidence adds to 1.7 bits, inside the uncertain band of 1 to 2 bits, so it is shown to the reviewer with the flag 'version differs' and is not merged. Results on 144 labelled pairs over 14 device families, each family held out in turn: no false merges in 60 non-matches, against 23 of 60 for a fuzzy-name baseline; precision 48 of 48. Recall is the weak number, 48 of 62: the 14 other true links are shown as ambiguous or not proposed. One reader wrote the labels; this is a development set.

## 16. A bounded loop that replans and critiques.

This is not a linear pipeline, and the slide says where it is and is not an agent loop. A bounded state machine runs roles: guard, discoverer, planner, retriever, extractor, resolver, compiler, critic, reporter. The planner delegates, and when the compiler finds a required dimension still unknown, the planner replans and reads more candidate records; the Transpara trace shows two such replans. The critic combines code checks with an adversarial Nemotron pass. State is shared through the World Model, H-MEM and a per-case budget that keeps its last three model calls for the planner, critic and reporter. Against the organizers' nine-point checklist, seven behaviours are visible in the recorded traces. Two are not: revision after a critic objection, because in these runs the critic confirmed no objection and changed no label, and a model or source fallback, which the drills exercise but the recorded traces do not show. The labels come from the compiler, not from any model.

## 17. Seven dimensions in, one of five labels out. No model.

The compiler is the distinguishing invention and it uses no model. It takes the intended use and the evidence scope, compares seven dimensions, each as contained, partial, disjoint or unknown, and a fixed table maps that to one of five labels. Authority depends on the kind of assertion: what use was authorized comes from the regulator's record and no study can widen it; what a study measured comes from the study report; what this hospital requires comes from its own policy; what the vendor says is only a claim to be checked. The controlled test changes exactly one dimension at a time on 18 real FDA summaries. 218 of 222 cases agree with the rule table, 24 of 24 positive controls come out supported, and none of the 198 cases whose key withholds support was labelled supported. That is a consistency result: it shows the code does what the table says, not that the table is clinically right. No standard prescribes deterministic applicability checks; the concepts are cited and the executable logic is our design inference. 17 of the 38 candidate registry rules are implemented, 17 more are ours, and 21 are not implemented, each with a reason.

## 18. Five labels. None of them says safe.

Five labels, and they are not a scale from worse to better. They describe what the evidence covers. The product never outputs safe, cleared or compliant; the label type is closed, the reporter rejects banned wording in code, and a request to certify something safe is blocked by the guard. Unknown is never false and never safe. A source that was down is never reported as a source that had nothing to say: those are different states with different words. On the recorded EyeArt case the ten vendor claims come out one supported, three supported only for a narrower use, one contradicted, two not enough evidence and three that need domain review. The guard blocked 25 of 34 certify-this requests in its own corpus and the other 9 were routed to clarification; the held-out figure is weaker and is on the safety slide.

On the left, the Claim X-ray for the claim that version 3.0 is covered: the answer, then each dimension with what the claim says beside what the evidence establishes and a button to open the exact passages, each with its page, source and retrieval date. On the right, the Applicability lab: the reviewer changes the software version to 3.0 and the answer recomputes in the browser from needs-domain-review to not-enough-evidence, with the one dimension that moved shown, and the badge says no model was called. ## 20. A review workstation, not a chat box.

Product and UX, in the judges' words: how intuitive, accessible and easy to use is the product for its intended users. The product is a review workstation, not a chat box: a review desk, a source console, and for each case an overview, the evidence matrix, the claim X-ray, the lab, a timeline, the investigation trace, the resilience drills and the dossier. Findings always use words and an icon, never colour alone. A banner on every screen states whether the run is a sample or live, and which parts are recorded. Automated checks cover axe in light, dark and phone layouts, keyboard-only flows, layouts from 360 to 1440 pixels and 200 percent zoom. What is missing is a moderated session with a real reviewer.

## 21. Seven drills. None turns a loss into support.

Reliability is shown by breaking things on purpose. There are seven drills, each run through the real code on the recorded evidence: a named source goes off, a named source answers with nothing, the federation engine stops, a federated result comes back truncated, the model route fails, a registry record changes after a finding, and a vendor document tells the system to mark the claim supported. The invariant is the same every time: a lost required source never produces a supported label, only the findings that relied on a changed fact go stale, a model that is off changes no label, and an instruction inside a document changes no label. 157 of 157 scripted drills end in the correct state, including stopping the real Zetaris API container, which the port reports as unavailable within milliseconds and which is answering again about 15 seconds after restart. This is a consistency result: the expected states come from the same builder. Not covered: the Nightshift route and the local lane were not faulted, and concurrent users were not tried.

## 22. Sign the run. One changed character fails it.

The output is a dossier: one signed bundle holding the claim, the intended use, every finding with its smallest mismatch and requirements, the source snapshots with their hashes, the Zetaris query ids and result hashes, the model receipts, the rule version and the reviewer decisions. An offline verifier recomputes every hash and runs the compiler again on the recorded inputs, and any mismatch fails with a named reason. The tamper test on the right: export, verify, change one character, and the verifier names the failing item. Be exact about what the signature means: it shows the bundle has not changed since the holder of the key signed it. It does not show that any finding is clinically correct, and this is a demo key that does not identify a signer.

## 23. Each requirement, its code path, its evidence.

Sponsor technology, row by row, in the organizers' own requirements, with the code path and the evidence for each. ## 24. The data layer: federation, policies, and what broke.

Zetaris is the data layer and it does real work. One statement over a Postgres hospital database, registry snapshot files and vendor files returned 20 rows in under a second on the real engine, identical cell by cell to an independent DuckDB run, and the comparison itself was checked: a changed cell and a missing row are both detected. Four identities see different columns for the same statement, and no confidential value reached the agent identity in 23 responses. It changes outputs: with the federation engine stopped every required finding becomes not enough evidence; and with the catalog-driven cross-source checks switched off, 16 of 48 mappable layouts are held for lack of corroboration and 4 layouts that must not register are registered. We also found defects and report them: Hide on a file-store table leaks the hidden value under the neighbouring column name; a datasource refresh deletes every policy on that source. Natural-language-to-SQL does not exist in this build: three routes were tried and are written down.

## 25. The layer saved no run time. It saved code.

The Zetaris judging core: onboard and query sources, build a Unified Semantic Layer and query it, add a data-quality rule, and reflect on how it saved time, how it helps and what could be better. The layer unifies ten tables and 89 columns across the three silos under the reviewer's own words, and is queried by the product as a read-only identity. We measured the same question three ways, nine timed rounds. Seven plain reads joined in code: 294 milliseconds. One hand-written federated statement: 861. One read of the layer: 1,189. The layer turned seven statements and 48 lines of join code into one statement and four lines, and it did not save run time: it is the slowest arm. Engineering time was not measured and no hours-saved figure is claimed. The data-quality rules caught a true problem: 143 of 157 registry records are search summaries with no eligibility block, so unknown is never read as covered. A deliberate truncation, 110 records cut to 39, failed the completeness rule and blocked that collection from feeding anything.

## 26. Each engine has a role, a result, and a caveat.

Four Meterless engines, each with a role in a working feature and a pre-registered ablation. Read the tags, because several results are by construction or weak, and the slide says which. H-MEM keeps reviewer-confirmed links, accepted mappings and precedents. On a constructed ten-case session, memory removed about half the repeated questions, from 22 to 10, but answered 4 questions with the answer to a different question, all identity links between similar-looking products; an identifier guard removes that hazard at the cost of one avoided question. Scoping by hospital means no hospital got the other's records; without it, 9 questions were answered from another hospital's records. The World Model marks only dependent findings stale: 9 recomputed instead of 36, none missed; the no-engine arm is zero flags by construction. Markovian reads a 40-page document in chunks: with the ids carryover it found the planted restriction in 3 of 4 documents against 2 of 4 for one full-length prompt, but only 4 documents were used, so one document is not evidence. Scout guards requests: 18 of 126 attacks got through against 105 of 126 without the injection layer, but on injection wording written after the rules were frozen, recall is 9 of 24 with rules alone, and the model classifier that raises it is off in the product.

## 27. Seven roles propose. Every reply is receipted.

Nemotron proposes; it does not decide. Seven roles, two models: Lightning 30B for the high-volume roles, Ultra 550B for identity adjudication and for the critic, which is deliberately a stronger and different model than the one that drafted. Nightshift is the primary route. All 61 recorded model replies of the committed run were answered through Nightshift and carry a signed compute receipt, and all 61 re-verify at start-up against a pinned key id. 29 were answered by Ultra and 32 by Lightning. The replayed run has every reply receipted: 14 of 14 for EyeArt, 23 of 23 each for Lunit and Transpara. Be exact about what a receipt shows: where a reply was routed and which model answered, not that it is true, and it carries no hash of the reply text. NVIDIA's own API answers only if Nightshift is unreachable or a receipt fails, and then the reply is labelled and unreceipted. The generic comparison arms called Lightning through NVIDIA's API directly, not through Nightshift.

## 28. Fetched text is data. The guard's weak number is on the slide.

Safety and security, with the weak number shown. Source fetching is allowlist-first and SSRF-safe: https and GET only, resolved addresses checked against private, loopback, link-local and cloud-metadata ranges, DNS pinned, every redirect re-checked, size and time capped; 21 of 21 SSRF drills end correctly. All fetched text is data and never enters the instruction channel; a vendor document that gives orders is held back and the exact words are set aside. The guard's honest numbers: on the dev wording the rules were tuned on, injection recall is 59 of 59; on three held-out files, 43 of 67; on wording written after the rules were frozen, 9 of 24. A model classifier raises that to 16 of 24 but also raises false blocks of legitimate requests above the gate, so the product runs the rules only. Data classes decide what may reach a model: public records yes; vendor-confidential content only with a local lane or recorded consent; patient data never, and none exists in the repository. Zetaris policies mask the vendor's commercial columns for identities not assigned to a case.

## 29. Five suites. The class of every result is printed beside it.

Six result sets in five suites, each with its class stated next to the number. Consistency means the code is compared with a rule table or specification the same builders wrote: it shows the code does what the table says and nothing about whether the table is clinically right. Builder-labelled means scored against labels one builder wrote from the source documents. Domain-validity would need qualified, blind reviewers; there are none, so this deck contains none. No sentence claims clinical or independent validation. The held-out set of 20 claims over five devices was sealed by file hash before any system ran, but the extraction layer was not blind to those five documents and later changes were made after the first held-out score, so the held-out numbers are a second look and the slides say so. The erratum log has seven entries; the first records that the Gate 0 answer key was edited after the builder saw a dry run of the compiler. Cases of one device are correlated, so intervals come from a bootstrap over devices, and the effective sample size is the number of devices.

## 30. It never over-claims. It is less often exactly right.

Every row of the same-input comparison, including the unfavourable ones. Three panels, two bars per arm: development and held-out. Left: how often the exact label is right. Sakaime is 9 of 26 on development and 9 of 20 held-out, which is below both generic Nemotron arms. Middle: how often an unsupported claim is labelled supported. Sakaime: none of 17 and none of 15. The generic arms do it for 20 to 39 percent, and keyword and source-by-source baselines for 53 to 77 percent. Right: the cost. On well-supported claims Sakaime raises a false alarm on 9 of 9 development claims and 4 of 5 held-out, because the source documents leave a dimension unplaced. So the advantage is that it does not over-claim, not exact-label accuracy. The comparisons are underpowered, 26 claims in 12 device families, and a p-value above 0.05 here is a tie, not equivalence. The generic arms ran three times for one call and once for the agent; means are shown.

## 31. Remove each part. Report what moved.

Every component has an ablation, and the status column says which are measured, which are by construction, which are null and which were not measured. Compiler off: the generic arms are more often exactly right and over-claim; that is the central trade. Compiler on complete hand-written evidence reaches 61.5 percent exact, but those items encode the key's own vocabulary, so it is an upper bound and not a result. Identity layer off: the same 9 of 26 exact labels, a null result on this metric; its effect shows in IdentityShift and in what reaches the compiler: of 53 candidate studies and papers, none of the 21 non-matches and none of the 11 ambiguous ones reach it. Cross-source checks off and model off both cost discovery outcomes. World Model off is by construction. H-MEM, Markovian and Scout are on constructed or small sets. Not measured: the critic role, in whose traces no label changed, the Nightshift receipts ablation, federation off as an accuracy ablation, and Zetaris natural language, which is blocked.

## 34. What this deck does not show.

Limits, stated before a judge has to find them. No clinical validation: the rules cite published concepts, but the executable logic and thresholds are our inference, and 21 of 38 candidate rules are not implemented. No buyer evidence and no reviewer study. One reader wrote every key, and the Gate 0 key was edited after a dry run; the erratum says so. In the public data only 1 of 12 devices has a study that states the version the regulator reviewed, so authentic mismatches are few. Extraction gaps are the main reason the full pipeline is conservative: population is unknown in 22 of the 26 development claims, results in stacked tables are not read, and a numeric performance claim is not supported by any case today. Every vendor claim is constructed. Sample mode replays recordings. The guard is weak on new wording. Receipts show routing, not truth. The Zetaris natural-language route is blocked and the layer saved no run time. Not run: a clean clone, Constat and HealthIT on held-out claims, cost and latency.

## 36. What would change what we can honestly say.

What comes next, in order of what would most change what we can honestly say. First, independent clinical or informatics reviewers labelling a held-out set blind: that is what turns a builder-labelled result into a domain-validity result. Second, buyer interviews with hospital AI intake committees, because demand is not measured. Third, extraction of stacked tables and of study populations and settings, which is the main reason the full pipeline is conservative. Fourth, more compiler rules as the evidence schema grows. Fifth, the Zetaris natural-language route when a build offers it. Sixth, domain packs: the same engine on a different kind of document, for example construction addenda or tariff revisions. That is a thesis, not a claim: only a pack that runs end to end with its own tests may be called working, and none does. The team is one builder, Prateek, working with Claude Code; Cursor was not used. The code is Apache-2.0 at the repository shown.
