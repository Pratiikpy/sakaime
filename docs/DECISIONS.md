## Decided during the build (2026-10-27)

Entries D-029 onward record choices made while the code was written. Each states the alternatives, the reason with its evidence file, and what would reverse it.

**D-029 (2026-10-27) Sources are registered as normalised snapshots only; REST-table registration is not built.** Alternatives: register each public API as a Zetaris REST table (the original plan, `03` s3). Reason: a REST table re-issues its request from inside the engine on every query (the gate counted the endpoint request rising from 13 to 15 after two queries), cannot apply the conversions the semantic checks recorded (a percent scale divided by 100, a date order), and would bypass the pinned fetch; large JSON also exhausts the driver heap (`infra/zetaris/GATES.md` Z-REST; `data/discovery-shift/RESULTS.md`, Limits). REST tables stay in the gates as a measured capability (a 3 KB endpoint answers in about 0.5 s) and are not used by the product. Reverse if a REST table can apply recorded conversions and pin the fetched bytes, or a source is too large for a snapshot.

**D-030 (2026-10-27) The Scout model classifier is optional and can only tighten a decision; the product's guard runs without it.** Alternatives: classifier always on; rules only; model in place of rules. Reason: on a held-out file written after the rules were frozen, injection recall is 9 of 24 with rules alone and 16 of 24 with the classifier, but the false-block rate on look-alike requests rises from 1 of 18 to 3 of 18 (6 of 48 across all held-out files, above the 10 percent gate), and on the external deepset set recall is 6.7 percent against 31.7 percent (`tests/engines/scout/RESULTS.md`). `src/agents/guard.ts` builds Scout with no classifier, so the product's guard operates at the rules-only figures: 43 of 67 held-out injections caught (64.2 percent), 9 of 24 on wording written after freeze. The classifier measures what a stronger guard would cost. Reverse if a classifier configuration meets the false-block gate on held-out look-alikes.

**D-031 (2026-10-27) A claim is bounded by the regulator's decision summary where the authorization is silent (rule `SAK-REGULATOR-BOUND`); the marker is a limitation line, and a first-class source field would be sturdier.** Alternatives: let studies and registry entries widen the population (the defect: an EyeArt claim for ages 18 and older compiled SUPPORTED_FOR_SCOPE because ClinicalTrials.gov and PubMed state 18 and above, while the FDA summary's pivotal analysis was of people aged 22 and older); bound by the authorization record only. Reason and correction of the premise: ERRATA E-006. The service marks study-typed items taken from the summary with the text `taken from the regulator's decision summary` in their free-text `limitations` (`src/compiler/pools.ts`, `src/service/assemble.ts`); the compiler narrows on that marker and never widens. A convention on free text is fragile: the evidence schema should carry a first-class `source` on the item so the extraction layer and the evaluation apply the rule without a string match. The evaluation adapter was changed to pass the marker (ERRATA E-007) and no reported number moved, so the rule is exercised by property tests (`tests/compiler/authority.test.ts`) and by one packet claim, not by the benchmark. Whether the regulator's analysed population should bound a claim at all is a design inference open to clinician challenge. Reverse on clinician advice, or replace the marker with a schema field.

**D-032 (2026-10-27) A study's population and care setting are not inherited from the indications.** Alternatives: copy the indications' conditions and settings onto the pivotal-study item. Reason: the indications say what the regulator permits, the study says who was measured; evidence items are grouped by subject and assertion type (`src/extraction/to-evidence.ts`), so a claim that rests on a study finds the results unattached to a population or setting the study record does not state. It is one of the reasons population is unknown in 22 of the 26 development claims and setting in 8 (`evals/RESULTS.md`, "Why V answers INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE so often", which also lists wording the compiler will not place), and the full pipeline confirms none of the nine well-supported controls. The cost is stated in the README. Reverse if inheritance can be shown to be sound for named document patterns, with a reviewer-visible record that it was inferred.

**D-033 (2026-10-27) Scout execution contracts are signed with HMAC-SHA256; dossiers and Nightshift receipts use Ed25519.** `02` s12 said Ed25519 for contracts; `04` s3.4 said HMAC. The sponsor specification and the contract tests follow `04`. Reason: a contract is an integrity check between modules of one deployment, with a five-minute expiry, scope and a one-time redemption; HMAC with a shared secret does that, and the code says it does not stop a party who holds the secret (`src/engines/scout/contract.ts`). The signer is an interface so an asymmetric scheme can replace it. Dossiers are signed with Ed25519 (a pinned key or a labelled demo key, `src/dossier/signer.ts`). Reverse if a judge or reviewer must verify a contract without holding the secret.

**D-034 (2026-10-27) The four engines are our own TypeScript implementations, checked against the sponsor's runners; only H-MEM and World Model references are vendored.** This refines D-008. Alternatives: extend the vendored references in place. Reason: the references are specification demonstrators with the gaps listed in `04`; our implementations in `src/engines/` pass the same sponsor conformance runners (H-MEM 13, World Model 8, Markovian 8), and the vendored files stay byte-for-byte with their hashes checked by `tests/engines/vendor-integrity.test.ts`. Markovian and Scout Intent are built from the sponsor's specification; the Markovian runner is invoked from a local clone and is not vendored; Scout has no sponsor runner, only a 32-example harness that exits 0. Reverse: no.

**D-035 (2026-10-27) The typecheck script reports but does not count diagnostics located in `third_party/`.** Alternatives: loosen our compiler settings to the vendored code; patch the vendored files. Reason: the vendored references are kept unmodified so conformance is neutral, and they do not pass our strict settings; `scripts/typecheck.mjs` checks every project of ours under strict settings and prints the vendored diagnostics as a count. Reverse if the vendored code is replaced by a typed release.

**D-036 (2026-10-27) The ten EyeArt packet claims and the hospital's intended use are constructed.** `input_examples/cases/eyeart-k200667.json` holds CLM-EA-01 to CLM-EA-10 and the intended use `CASE-2026-014.intended`; the regulator, registry and publication records they are checked against are real. The case file carries a `constructedNotice` shown on screen. Nothing is a statement by Eyenuk, Inc. The Lunit and Transpara cases are the same kind. Reverse: no.

**D-037 (2026-10-27) Gate 0 results are reported as they came out, including the key contamination.** The answer key was edited after the builder saw a dry run of the compiler (ERRATA E-001); no earlier version is recoverable. All 26 Gate 0 claims are labelled development in every table; a second set of 20 claims over five devices was written and sealed by hash before any system ran (`evals/SEALED.md`). Later changes to the adapter, the extraction layer and the compiler were made after the first held-out score, so the published held-out numbers are a second look (E-002, E-003, E-005, E-006). Alternatives: discard the 26 claims; hide the edit. Reverse: no.

**D-038 (2026-10-27) 21 of the 38 candidate registry rules are not implemented, and each is listed with its reason.** The current count is the number of keys of `UNIMPLEMENTED_REGISTRY_RULES` in `src/compiler/rules.ts` (R03, R04, R05, R06, R07, R12, R16, R17, R18, R22, R23, R26, R27, R28, R29, R30, R31, R35, R36, R37, R38); 17 registry rules are implemented (R01, R02, R08 to R11, R13 to R15, R19 to R21, R24, R25, R32 to R34) beside 17 rules of our own. Earlier drafts said 22; the code is the source. The unimplemented rules need fields the evidence schema does not carry (for example the adaptation mode, the number of sites, the evaluated role). Reverse by extending the schema and the extraction layer, one rule at a time, each with its test.

**D-039 (2026-10-27, superseded 2026-10-28) The first recorded run was made through the NVIDIA API.** It carried no receipts (14 replies, none receipted). It was replaced by a re-recording through Nightshift 1.2.3: all 61 recorded replies carry a signed receipt that re-verifies under the pinned key in `data/sample/receipt-key.json` (`output_examples/model-route.json`; dossier `receiptCoverage` 14 of 14, 23 of 23, 23 of 23). Nightshift is the primary route and the NVIDIA API the fallback (`src/models/stack.ts`).

## Where our reading differs from the outside documents

- The outside note frames the flagship as settled; we mark it CONDITIONAL until G1 and G2 pass.
- It names Z3, PydanticAI, Evidently and Sigstore as candidates; we adopt none until a need is shown (principle: a mechanism, not a dependency).
- It lists "Reddit search for practitioner workflow" in r/medicine and similar; Reddit access works only through a read-only front end here and healthcare communities are small for this topic. A buyer or reviewer interview is the stronger evidence and is still missing.
- It recommends running the evaluation with independent domain reviewers; none is secured. Until one is, results are labelled builder-adjudicated.
